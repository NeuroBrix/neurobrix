What the directory holds

One file per kernel and per dtype of the buffer the kernel writes, indexed by the launcher's shape key. Every entry carries its own proof: the date, the engine and backend versions, the shape, the deviation measured against the fp64 oracle, the profile's tolerance, and the machine.

The catalogue

Ordered by known cost ascending — the position is a queue position, not a rank. Five models have a recorded cost and lead; the rest are ordered by weight, a declared crude proxy whose only job is to try cheap things first. What protects a night's budget is not that ordering but the guard: a cell whose known cost exceeds what remains is refused at the door, and a cell with no known cost is never refused on a guess.

The columns that are data, not a score

certified — how many of the keys this model demands the directory already holds. differ — on how many of them the runtime's own sweep picked a different setting from the certified one. A large differ is not alarming by itself, which is what the next two columns separate.

near-tie — the difference sits inside the certifier's own margin: its second-best was within a few percent of its best, so the runtime's pick is the timer's noise and not a disagreement. contradicted — the runtime chose differently on a key with a clear margin. That is a finding every time, one of the two things the certified-directory doctrine says must never be a silence.

screened out — configurations the consensus screen excluded before timing, each with its kernel, key and deviation in the run's log. An exclusion is data: it says how contested a target's configuration space is.

The decomposition is checked, not assumed: differ must equal near-tie + contradicted + excluded-picked + uncertified. It closes on every measured row.

What the measured rows say, and what they do not

Median gain ×2.39 over five cells. That number is inseparable from its population, which is exactly these models and no others:

It may not be compared with a median over a different population. An earlier campaign reported ×13.9 over a set that included chatterbox (674 keys) and openaudio (693); this one does not carry them. Saying “the median fell” against that number would describe which models were run, in the grammar of a statement about the engine.

The law these rows show is the key count, not the model size: 54 GB at 203 keys gains ×11.69 while 61 GB at 8 keys gains ×1.54. The table is ordered by cost — read the keys column for the explanation of any gain.

Why the unmeasured rows have no estimate

A static count of distinct autotuned-op shapes, read from each container's graph.json, was tried as a predictor of the key count and does not work: two containers with 54 static shapes each measured 8 and 137 keys, and the ratio of measured to static ranges from 0.15 to 2.54 across the five known cells. It is not a weak predictor; it is not a predictor. Rather than fill the column with it, the column stays empty.
